# Marathon Preparation

## Training Phases

A well-structured marathon preparation typically spans **16-20 weeks** and is divided into four phases:

**Phase 1 — Base Building (weeks 1-6)**
Building aerobic base and weekly mileage. 80% of runs at easy conversational pace (zone 1-2). Weekly mileage increases by no more than 10% per week — the 10% rule, endorsed by coaches including Jeff Galloway and Hal Higdon. Typical weekly volume: 50-70 km for intermediate runners.

**Phase 2 — Development (weeks 7-12)**
Introduction of quality sessions: tempo runs, progression runs, and medium-long runs with marathon pace segments. Weekly mileage peaks at 70-100 km for recreational marathoners, 140-200 km for elites.

**Phase 3 — Peak (weeks 13-16)**
Highest training stress. Includes the longest long run (28-35 km), back-to-back long runs on weekends (used by coaches like Pfitzinger), and race-pace marathon segments within long runs.

**Phase 4 — Taper (weeks 17-20)**
Gradual mileage reduction while maintaining intensity. Weekly volume drops by 20-25% each week in the final 3 weeks. Research shows taper improves race performance by 2-3% through glycogen supercompensation and muscle repair.

## Key Training Sessions

**The Long Run**
The cornerstone of marathon training. Run at 60-90 seconds per km slower than goal marathon pace. Increases mitochondrial density, fat oxidation efficiency, and glycogen storage capacity. Most plans cap the long run at 35 km — running longer increases injury risk without proportional benefit.

**Tempo Run (Lactate Threshold)**
Run at a pace you could sustain for approximately 60 minutes in a race. Typically 20-40 minutes at threshold pace. Raises the speed at which lactate accumulates — the primary physiological driver of marathon performance. Jack Daniels defines tempo pace as approximately 25-30 seconds per km slower than 10K race pace.

**Marathon Pace Runs**
Segments at goal marathon pace within longer runs, e.g. 10-16 km at marathon pace within a 25 km run. Teaches the body to run economically at race pace and builds mental familiarity with the target effort.

**Easy Recovery Runs**
50-60% of weekly volume. Heart rate below 75% of max. Essential for aerobic adaptation — most physiological gains from hard sessions occur during recovery, not during the session itself.

## Pace Calculation

**Basic marathon pace formula:**
- 3:00 marathon = 4:16 min/km
- 3:30 marathon = 4:58 min/km
- 4:00 marathon = 5:41 min/km
- 4:30 marathon = 6:24 min/km
- 5:00 marathon = 7:06 min/km

**Predicting marathon time from shorter races:**
- 5K time × 9.0 = approximate marathon time
- 10K time × 4.65 = approximate marathon time
- Half marathon time × 2.1 = approximate marathon time (accounting for fatigue)

These multipliers assume adequate marathon-specific training. Runners who undertrain for the distance typically see larger multipliers.

## Nutrition During Training

**Carbohydrate loading** begins 3 days before the race. Increase carbohydrate intake to 10-12 g per kg of body weight daily while reducing training volume (taper). This maximizes muscle glycogen stores — a 70 kg runner can store approximately 500 g of glycogen, providing energy for roughly 30-32 km of marathon running.

**During training runs over 75 minutes:** consume 30-60 g of carbohydrates per hour. Options: energy gels, dates, bananas, sports drinks. Practice race-day nutrition during training — never try new products on race day.

**Fat adaptation training:** some advanced runners perform fasted long runs (up to 25 km without carbohydrates) to improve fat oxidation efficiency. This is an advanced strategy — not recommended for beginners.

## Taper Strategy

The taper is frequently mismanaged. Common mistakes:

- **Too much reduction** — cutting mileage by 50%+ leaves the body feeling flat and sluggish
- **Too little reduction** — carrying fatigue into race day
- **Eliminating quality** — maintain intensity during taper, only reduce volume
- **Taper tantrums** — normal psychological symptoms: restlessness, self-doubt, phantom pains. Recognize them as a sign the taper is working.

Optimal taper: reduce volume by 20-25% per week for 3 weeks. Keep 2-3 quality sessions per week including short marathon pace segments. Last hard workout no later than 10 days before the race.

## Race Day Strategy

**Pacing:** negative splitting (running the second half faster than the first) is associated with better finishing times. Start 5-10 seconds per km slower than goal pace for the first 5 km.

**The wall:** glycogen depletion typically occurs around km 30-32. Proper carbohydrate intake during the race (60-90 g/hour with multiple carbohydrate sources) delays or prevents bonking.

**Weather adjustment:** for every 5°C above 12°C, adjust goal pace upward by approximately 2-3%. Running a 3:30 goal time in 22°C conditions — target 3:35-3:37.

## Strength Training for Marathon Runners

**Key exercises:**
- Single-leg squats and lunges — gluteal strength reduces knee and hip injury risk
- Calf raises (single leg, slow eccentric) — Achilles tendon resilience
- Hip flexor strengthening — running economy at marathon pace
- Core stability (plank variations, dead bugs) — maintains form in late race miles

2 sessions per week of 30-40 minutes during base and development phases. Reduce to 1 session during peak and taper phases.

## Recovery After a Marathon

Full muscular recovery takes **3-4 weeks**. The commonly cited rule: 1 easy day per mile raced (26 easy days after a marathon). Elite runners typically return to full training in 2-3 weeks; recreational runners should allow 4-6 weeks.

Post-marathon recovery protocol:
- Days 1-3: rest, walking only, prioritize sleep
- Days 4-7: easy walking, light stretching, no running
- Week 2: easy running up to 30 minutes if pain-free
- Week 3-4: gradual return to normal easy running volume
- Week 5+: reintroduce quality sessions
